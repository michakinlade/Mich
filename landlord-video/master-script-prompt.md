# MASTER SCRIPT PROMPT: Money-Narrative Channel (Dark Ledger style)

Copy everything below the line. Fill in the [BRACKETS] and paste it into any AI.

---

You are a scriptwriter for a faceless YouTube channel that tells money stories in 2D animation. The channel is about how money quietly changes the way systems and people treat you. It gives no financial advice and no how-to steps. Write ONE complete, original, voiceover-ready script.

## INPUTS
- Topic: [TOPIC, e.g. "How hospitals treat you at every level of wealth"]
- Format: [A = Level Ladder | B = Life Drama]
- Target length: [WORD COUNT, e.g. 2,300] words (stay within ±5%)
- Optional details: [names, ages, numbers, settings, twist you want]

## POINT OF VIEW AND VOICE
- Second person, present tense, 100% of the time. The viewer is the protagonist ("You open the app. The number has a minus sign.").
- The protagonist is never named. Supporting characters always get simple first names (Victor, Priya, Ryan, Maya).
- The narrator is calm, intimate and slightly clinical, like someone who has watched this happen a thousand times. Warm toward people, cold toward systems.
- Never label emotions loudly. Name the missing or unexpected feeling instead ("Not anger. Just tired." / "You don't feel rich. You feel behind.").
- No intro, no "welcome back", no channel name, no call to subscribe, no advice, no lists of tips, no headings, no emojis.

## SENTENCE RHYTHM
- Mostly short declaratives of 4–10 words. Use fragments freely ("Non-refundable." "Thin at the elbows now.").
- Every 5–7 lines, add one long cascading sentence of 25–40 words joined with "and…, and…, and…".
- Use negation runs: "Not X. Not Y. Just Z." / "No warning. No call. No explanation."
- Repeat for weight: "Quiet. Waiting. Quiet."
- End most paragraphs on a 3–6 word punch.
- Numbers are always exact and slightly odd ($4, $35, $3,200, 6.8%, 7:12 on a Friday), except for mythic anchors ($40, 13 years).

## HOOK (first 150 words)
1. Open mid-scene with a specific time, an exact dollar amount, and one physical object.
2. Set a mundane action against a massive hidden fact or an unfair system.
3. Land the first twist or impossibility within 60–120 words.
4. Deliver the thesis line within 150–250 words as forbidden knowledge ("Here's the part nobody prints…", "Here's what the number never warned you about…").
5. Format B only: add a promise line ("This is how ___ became ___. And why ___ got harder every year.").

## FORMAT A: LEVEL LADDER (7–9 levels)
- Each level opens with the header spoken aloud: "Level [N], $[amount]."
- Each level follows this loop:
  1. A state change ("Something shifts. Not in X. In Y.")
  2. Two or three concrete micro-scenes (the app, the counter, the phone call, the letter)
  3. A callback comparing this level to an earlier one ("The same ___ that ignored you at $200 just ___ at $2,000.")
  4. A realization ("And you realize something…")
  5. A closing verdict: "Rule [N]. At $[amount], you're not ___. You're ___."
- Low levels get painful, sensory detail. High levels get abstraction, access and influence.
- Plant 3–4 objects or numbers at Level 1 (a fee, a phone battery, a hallway, a drip) and echo them at higher levels as memories.
- Outro: "Tonight, somewhere…" replays the Level 1 scene with a new stranger in third person. Close on a single thesis line about identity versus the number.

## FORMAT B: LIFE DRAMA (one protagonist, 10–15 years)
- Cold open at the story's end point, with a secret the protagonist is keeping.
- Flash back to the origin with exact age, salary, savings, debt and net worth.
- An origin ritual (a photo, a question at a table, a small loan, a gift) that pays off at the end.
- A quiet decision: one small, secret rule made once.
- Three to four acts of compressed years, each with 2–3 dramatized scenes and ending on a foreshadowing line ("You had no way of knowing…", "You'll remember it for 13 years.").
- A false resolution ("And for a while, it works.") right before the collapse.
- A dark turn in which the money or the secret starts to corrupt a relationship, and the protagonist notices something ugly in themselves.
- The climax returns to the cold-open moment and the secret is finally shown or spoken.
- The ending echoes the origin image with new meaning and closes on a quiet triad or a shared silence.

## RETENTION RULES
- Drop a quotable aphorism every 60–90 seconds (about every 150–230 words) that could stand alone as a clip ("The penalty for being broke is that you become more broke.").
- Every section ends on an open loop or a forward-looking line.
- Cut scenes before they fully resolve.
- Keep numbers escalating so the viewer tracks the score.
- Keep 2–3 recurring motifs and pay each one off.
- Include at least one tender, unspoken-love moment and at least one moment of moral discomfort for the protagonist.

## ORIGINALITY
- Every sentence must be newly written. Match the style, never the wording, of any existing video.
- Use a fresh setting, fresh names and fresh numbers every time.

## OUTPUT
1. First line: "TARGET: [N] words".
2. The full script as plain paragraphs, voiceover-ready (no scene directions, no brackets, no visual notes).
3. Last line: "FINAL: [N] words · est. runtime [N/150] min".

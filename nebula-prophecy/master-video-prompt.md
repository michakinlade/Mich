## PART 1: SCRIPT

**The core formula (every story uses it, in a new world each time)**
- **Lead:** an ordinary, capable human pushed into a forbidden place. They speak plainly, in modern English, and are often afraid.
- **Creature or guardian:** a powerful being the lead is told to fear. It turns out to be the protector or victim, never the true monster.
- **Villain:** humans with power (an empire, a crown, an elder, slavers, traders). They are arrogant, openly contemptuous, and say what they want out loud.
- **MacGuffin:** a named object with a mythic job. Introduce it early and explain it late.
- **Pact:** the lead and the creature join through a symbol, scent, blood, an elixir or a bond without words.
- **Family stake:** a sibling, parent or child is taken, lost or waiting at home.

**Structure: cold open + 6 chapters titled "CHAPTER N: TITLE" (each about 1/6 of the words)**
- **COLD OPEN:** one out-of-context character line before any setup, then [music].
- **CH 1, Trespass:** someone warns that no one survives the forbidden place. The lead is pushed in, with a family moment (a promise or token). End with the first sign of the creature or the first attack.
- **CH 2, Confrontation:** "Who are you?", then an accusation and a denial. Name the MacGuffin. Plant the conditional line: "If you want ___, you must do something for me." Don't explain it yet.
- **CH 3, Backstory:** an object triggers a first-person flashback: the peaceful life, then "until they came." The villain gets a 2–4 line sneering monologue.
- **CH 4, Assault:** battle commands in short bursts, then a capture and an either/or ultimatum. The truth is overheard or seen.
- **CH 5, Revenge or rescue:** the creature's power is revealed and the villains pay. A one-line vow. The pact. A brave act with the last resource.
- **CH 6, Payoff:** repeat the conditional line word for word. Reward, transformation or homecoming, and the villain is exposed or exiled. End on a short line that reframes the MacGuffin, or cut off mid-mission.

**Dialogue rules**
- Dialogue only. No narrator, except a character's first-person flashback.
- Lines are 3–12 words (up to 16 in the flashback). Use parallel openings, triads, either/or ultimatums, short proclamations, and one echo line repeated early and late.
- **Voices:**
  - The lead is plain and modern.
  - The guardian is grave and lightly old-fashioned.
  - The villains mock and call people "savages" or "fools."
- The creature communicates only through sound cues.
- Simple global English. No viewer address, no channel name, no call to subscribe, no emojis.
- Do not describe visuals in the script.

**Pacing**
- About 0.5 spoken words per second overall. About 55–60% of the runtime has no speech.
- Put sound cues on their own lines between most dialogue moments. Use only: [music], [screaming], [panting], [breathing], [roar], [crash], [soft cry], [singing], [laughter].
- An action spike every 2–3 minutes.

**Output**
- Target word count, then the script (CHARACTER: line, cues in brackets, chapters separated by ---).
- Then the final spoken word count, which must be within ±5% of the target. Revise until it is.

## LOCKED VISUAL STYLE (applies to Parts 3–5)

Start every image prompt with this exact style tag:
> Photorealistic cinematic film still, anamorphic 2.39:1 widescreen, shallow depth of field, desaturated teal-green color grade, low-key moody lighting with soft rim light, atmospheric haze, subtle film grain, high micro-detail on skin, dirt and textures, gritty survival-thriller mood, Hollywood blockbuster production quality.

- Looks like live action with real actors. Never illustrated, stylized or cartoonish.
- **Palette:** moss green, olive, wet brown and slate blue, with warm skin tones and small accents (amber eyes, gold, blood, fire).
- **Lighting:** low-key and motivated (canopy light, firelight, torches, moonlight, practical LEDs), with haze for depth.
- **Camera:** 35–85mm feel, eye-level or slightly low. Sometimes shot through foreground leaves or objects as if watching from hiding. Close-ups for emotion, wide shots for scale.
- **Detail:** mud, sweat, scratches, wet hair and worn wardrobe.
- **Flashbacks** only: warmer, hazier golden light with soft bloom.

## PART 3: BEAT SHEET + IMAGE PROMPTS

**Beat rules**
- Every beat is 5 seconds or less. Time dialogue beats at 2.3 words per second and split any line longer than 11 words into two beats.
- Every dialogue line and every sound cue gets at least one beat. Nothing is skipped.
- Each chapter opens with a title-card beat: a wide establishing shot with the chapter title as a clean white serif overlay in the lower third.
- **Fill the silence to hit the runtime:** total beats ≈ target runtime in seconds ÷ 4.5. Split every [music] cue into as many 5-second silent-action beats as needed (for example [music] (1/6) … (6/6)). Show the journey, the atmosphere, reaction shots, the creature half-hidden and villain activity. Put more beats into chapter openings and action scenes.
- Vary the shot sizes. Never use the same camera angle three beats in a row.

**For each beat, output:**
- **Beat NNN · start–end (duration)**
- **[Script Segment]:** the exact line with the speaker, or the cue with its shot number
- **Image Prompt:** fully standalone, in this order:
  1. The style tag
  2. Setting: the bible location line
  3. Characters: the full bible block for everyone in frame
  4. Scene: one clear action
  5. Camera, then lighting, then mood
  6. "No text, no watermark, no logos." (title cards allow only the chapter title)
- **Camera Angle / Lighting / Mood / Action:** one line each

Never write "same as before" or refer to another prompt. Each prompt must work alone.

## PART 4: VIDEO PROMPTS (image-to-video)

Write one prompt per beat. The beat's generated image is the start frame. Round the clip length up to 2–5 seconds.

Template:
> Image-to-video, [N] seconds. Animate the provided frame as a photorealistic cinematic live-action shot: anamorphic 2.39:1, shallow depth of field, desaturated teal-green grade, subtle film grain, no style drift, no morphing of faces, bodies or the creature. Motion: [the beat's action as movement]. [Performance line]. [Lip line]. [Creature line]. Ambient motion: [location-specific: mist, embers, leaves, water, smoke]. Camera: [movement]. Lighting stays consistent: [beat lighting]. Pacing and mood: [mood]. No new characters, no cuts, no on-screen text, no audio.

- **Performance line:**
  - Calm beats: "Movement is grounded and realistic, with small natural micro-expressions and breathing."
  - Action cues ([crash], [roar], [screaming]): "Motion is fast and physical, with real weight and momentum; debris, mud and sparks react naturally."
- **Lip line (dialogue beats only):** "The speaker's lips move naturally as if saying: "[line]"."
- **Creature line (when it's in frame):** "Its camouflage or skin ripples subtly with shifting colors."
- **Camera movement by shot type:**
  - Close-up: slow push-in
  - Insert or extreme close-up: very slow macro push-in
  - Wide shot: slow lateral drift
  - Establishing or high shot: slow descending crane
  - Low angle: slow upward tilt
  - Two-shot or over-the-shoulder: gentle arc
  - Through foreground: slow slide past foreground elements
  - Action: fast handheld tracking with a slight shake
- **Chapter cards:** the title fades in softly in the lower third and stays sharp.

## PART 5: THUMBNAILS + PACKAGING

**5 thumbnails.** For each, give:
- A visual concept
- The text overlay (always "None", because the channel uses no thumbnail text)
- The emotion trigger
- A style-matched prompt

**Thumbnail style (different from the video frames):**
- Bright, crisp HDR daylight or bright moonlight, everything in sharp focus, 16:9, no text.
- **Face-off composition:** the human sits low and vulnerable on one third (sitting, kneeling, half-sunk in mud or water, hurt or holding a small blade). The creature is full-body and clearly readable on the other third, approaching or looming, with an empty gap between them.
- Muted earth palette. Warm skin against cool or grey backgrounds. One bright accent (amber eyes, gold, red eyes, blood).
- Wet, glistening textures and a dramatic sky or mist.
- **Triggers:** "Will it attack or help?", beauty and the beast, awe at the scale, primal danger.
- Show what makes THIS story unique, so it doesn't look like the channel's earlier videos.

**Titles:** 3 options in the channel formula: a setup, an ellipsis, then a twist that gets cut off. Use hooks like "They Sent Him to ___…", "They Left Him to ___…" and "Beautiful ___ Finds ___…". Put the twist after the ellipsis so it gets cut off in the feed. Example: "They Sent Him to Kill the Beast… But It Knew His Mother".

**Description:** 3–4 sentences. A punchy hook line with one emoji, a line setting up the premise, a tease of the secret, and the ending "Watch until the very end to find out!"
